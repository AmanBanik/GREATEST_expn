## 1. Question and Pre-Registered Hypotheses

### Research Question
How does the elapsed wall-clock time per dependent pointer traversal change as the allocated working set increases from small sizes (16 KiB) up to 64 MiB?

### Pre-Registered Hypotheses (from Protocol Rev 3.0)
- **H1:** For sizes $\le 32$ KiB, average time per step will remain low and relatively flat (~1–4 ns/step), reflecting high hit rates in primary on-core cache storage.
- **H2:** As working-set size increases from 16 KiB to 64 MiB, elapsed time per operation will increase, exhibiting one or more upward inflection regions consistent with memory hierarchy constraints.
- **H3:** At large working sets (> 16 MiB), the pointer chain spans thousands of 4 KiB virtual pages, potentially incurring address translation overheads (TLB misses / page-table walks) alongside cache miss penalties.

## 2. Experimental Design & Parameters

- **Mode:** `chase` (single-chain dependent pointer traversal)
- **Independent Variable:** Working-set size (13 primary sizes: 16 KiB to 64 MiB; 2 post-sweep verification sizes: 16 KiB and 1 MiB)
- **Dependent Variable:** Elapsed wall-clock time (`seconds`) and derived nanoseconds per operation (`ns_per_operation`)
- **Controlled Variables:**
  - `WORK_COUNT`: Uniformly fixed at `2,000,000` steps across all sizes
  - `REPEATS`: `5` timed repetitions per size
  - `SEED`: `12345` (deterministic random generator)
  - `CHAINS`: `1` (single chain)
- **Element Size:** `sizeof(size_t) == 8` bytes per pointer node
- **Warm-Up:** Untimed warm-up call (`result_sink = kernel();`) executed before repetition 0
- **Dead-Code Elimination:** Accumulator stored in `static volatile u64 result_sink`

## 7. Evidence Separation & Scientific Interpretation

### A. Observed (Directly Present in Captured Output)
1. At 16 KiB and 32 KiB, median elapsed time per step was 1.47 ns and 1.24 ns, with small variation (IQR $\le 0.25$ ns).
2. Between 32 KiB and 64 KiB, median latency increased from 1.24 ns to 2.63 ns ($\sim 2.1\times$).
3. Between 2 MiB and 4 MiB, median latency stepped up from 6.19 ns to 13.71 ns ($\sim 2.2\times$).
4. Between 8 MiB and 16 MiB, median latency stepped up from 16.31 ns to 40.98 ns ($\sim 2.5\times$).
5. Between 16 MiB, 32 MiB, and 64 MiB, median latency rose from 40.98 ns to 88.16 ns and reached 100.32 ns.
6. The post-sweep verification re-test yielded medians of 1.67 ns (16 KiB) and 4.57 ns (1 MiB), closely matching the primary sweep medians of 1.47 ns and 4.61 ns.
7. Checksums were 100% identical across all 5 repetitions for every single size.

### B. Calculated (Derived Mathematical Results)
1. Latency ratio from smallest to largest working set:
   $$\frac{\text{Median}(64\text{ MiB})}{\text{Median}(32\text{ KiB})} = \frac{100.3209\text{ ns}}{1.2392\text{ ns}} \approx 80.96\times$$
   Dependent pointer step latency increases by a factor of $\sim 81\times$ as the allocated working set scales from 32 KiB to 64 MiB.
2. Hysteresis drift across the sweep:
   $$\Delta_{1\text{ MiB}} = |4.6121 - 4.5709| = 0.0412\text{ ns} \quad (0.9\% \text{ deviation})$$
   $$\Delta_{16\text{ KiB}} = |1.4735 - 1.6682| = 0.1947\text{ ns} \quad (13.2\% \text{ deviation})$$
   Demonstrates high reproducibility across the multi-second execution window.

### C. Interpretation (Consistent Explanatory Models)
1. **Support for H1 (L1d Plateau):** The low, flat latency at 16 KiB and 32 KiB (~1.2–1.5 ns) is consistent with the hypothesis of high L1d cache hit rates. At typical modern CPU clock frequencies (~4.5–5.0 GHz), 1.2–1.5 ns corresponds to $\sim 5$–8 clock cycles, which aligns well with typical L1 load-to-use latency.
2. **Support for H2 (Multi-Stage Regimes):** The observed step changes closely track the nominal cache hierarchy boundaries reported by `lscpu`:
   - First step: between 32 KiB and 64 KiB (near the 48 KiB L1d boundary).
   - Second step: between 2 MiB and 4 MiB (near the 2 MiB L2 boundary).
   - Third step: past 8 MiB and 16 MiB, rising sharply towards 32 MiB and 64 MiB (near and beyond the 30 MiB L3 boundary).
3. **Consistency with H3 (TLB & DRAM Latencies):** The latency plateau of $\sim 88$–$100$ ns at 32 MiB and 64 MiB is consistent with the compound cost of L3 misses, virtual memory translation across thousands of pages (TLB misses), and DRAM access round-trip times.

### D. Hypothesis Status
- **H1:** **Supported** by data.
- **H2:** **Supported** by data (clear multi-stage regimes observed).
- **H3:** **Consistent** with data; however, timing alone cannot isolate the TLB component from the memory controller component.

### E. Unknowns & Critical Guardrails
- **No PMU Validation:** We did not measure hardware cache misses or bus transactions. We do not claim measured DRAM traffic or proven cache-miss rates.
- **No Direct TLB Isolation:** The exact fraction of latency at 64 MiB caused by TLB misses vs. DRAM column access strobe (CAS) latency remains unknown.
- **Physical Core Scheduling:** WSL2 reports a virtualized view; whether execution ran on physical P-cores or E-cores remains unverified from within the guest.
