## 1. Research Question & Pre-Registered Hypotheses

### Research Context & Question
In EXP-20261016-02, multi-chain pointer traversal revealed dramatic amortized speedup under concurrency at 64 MiB ($3.56\times$–$4.71\times$), but also uncovered significant launch-to-launch execution variance (verification sweeps showed up to $+40.22\%$ drift at 32 KiB and $+37.07\%$ at 64 MiB).

EXP-20261016-03 investigates three core questions under a rigorous, 5-block randomized interleaved execution schedule with separate process launches and procedural pauses:
1. Does the multi-chain kernel (`chains` mode with $C=1$) introduce substantial execution overhead relative to single-chain pointer chasing (`chase` mode) at the 32 KiB tier?
2. Does interleaving configurations with separate process launches and inter-launch pauses moderate launch-to-launch variability and temporal drift compared to continuous sequential sweeps?
3. Does concurrent pointer traversal at the 64 MiB tier achieve an intra-block amortized speedup exceeding $3.00\times$ ($C=4$ vs. $C=1$) consistently across all independent execution blocks?

### Pre-Registered Hypotheses
- **H7 (Kernel Equivalence Heuristic — 32 KiB):**  
  At 32 KiB (4,096 elements), the median amortized latency per operation for `chains` with $C=1$ will fall within $\pm 5.0\%$ of the median for `chase` mode:
  $$-5.0\% \le \frac{\text{Median}(\text{chains}, C=1) - \text{Median}(\text{chase})}{\text{Median}(\text{chase})} \le +5.0\%$$
  *Methodological Qualification:* This criterion serves as a pre-registered descriptive heuristic check across balanced blocks ($N=25$ each). It indicates practical comparability and does not constitute formal statistical equivalence (e.g., two one-sided tests) or inferential proof of zero runtime overhead.
- **H8 (Launch-to-Launch Dispersion & Drift Moderation):**  
  Separate process launches with inter-launch procedural pauses ($2.0\text{ s}$) and randomized block interleaving will moderate between-block dispersion compared to the extreme verification drift observed in continuous sweeps ($+40.22\%$ at 32 KiB in EXP-02).  
  *Epistemological Guardrail:* EXP-02 and EXP-03 employ structurally different execution designs. Bounded dispersion in EXP-03 is an informative descriptive finding, not causal proof that process relaunches or interleaving alone produced the difference.
- **H9 (Within-Block Concurrency Speedup — 64 MiB):**  
  Within every single block $b \in \{1, \dots, 5\}$, the amortized operation speedup for $C=4$ relative to $C=1$ will strictly exceed $3.00\times$:
  $$S_b = \frac{\text{Median}(64\text{M } C=1)_b}{\text{Median}(64\text{M } C=4)_b} > 3.00\times \quad \forall b \in \{1, 2, 3, 4, 5\}$$
  *Clarification:* Speedup reflects amortized wall-clock throughput per pointer operation under concurrent memory access. It does not measure physical DRAM load latency directly.

## 7. Detailed Hypothesis Evaluations

### Hypothesis H7 — Kernel Equivalence Heuristic (32 KiB Tier)
- **Metric:** Percentage difference between overall median 32 KiB `chains` ($C=1$) and 32 KiB `chase`:
  $$\Delta_{\text{kernel}} = \frac{0.988748 - 0.955430}{0.955430} = +3.4872\% \quad (\approx +3.49\%)$$
- **Evaluation Criterion:** $-5.0\% \le \Delta_{\text{kernel}} \le +5.0\%$
- **Result:** **SUPPORTED (Practical Equivalence)**
- **Interpretation:** The single-chain instantiation of the multi-chain kernel (`chains` with $C=1$) adds minimal execution overhead ($+3.49\%$) relative to the dedicated single-pointer chasing kernel (`chase`), falling comfortably within the $\pm 5.0\%$ heuristic tolerance band.
- *Limitation:* This comparison is a descriptive heuristic based on $N=25$ repetitions per mode across 5 blocks. It does not establish formal equivalence boundaries or prove zero overhead at the machine instruction level.

---

### Hypothesis H8 — Launch-to-Launch Variability & Between-Block Dispersion
- **Observed Metrics across Block Medians:**
  - **Config 1 (32 KiB `chase`):** Range $[0.9353, 1.0108]\text{ ns}$, Spread $0.0754\text{ ns}$, Mean $0.9658\text{ ns}$, Std $0.0305\text{ ns}$, CV $3.16\%$, Max Drift **$8.06\%$**.
  - **Config 2 (32 KiB `chains` $C=1$):** Range $[0.9471, 1.0108]\text{ ns}$, Spread $0.0636\text{ ns}$, Mean $0.9760\text{ ns}$, Std $0.0253\text{ ns}$, CV $2.59\%$, Max Drift **$6.72\%$**.
  - **Config 3 (64 MiB `chains` $C=1$):** Range $[76.0008, 93.7601]\text{ ns}$, Spread $17.7593\text{ ns}$, Mean $81.6829\text{ ns}$, Std $7.4152\text{ ns}$, CV $9.08\%$, Max Drift **$23.37\%$**.
  - **Config 4 (64 MiB `chains` $C=4$):** Range $[18.0925, 22.2419]\text{ ns}$, Spread $4.1494\text{ ns}$, Mean $19.8705\text{ ns}$, Std $1.7980\text{ ns}$, CV $9.05\%$, Max Drift **$22.93\%$**.
- **Contextual Comparison:**  
  In EXP-02, continuous sequential sweeps resulted in verification drift of $+40.22\%$ at 32 KiB and $+37.07\%$ at 64 MiB. In EXP-03, 32 KiB launch-to-launch drift was bounded within $6.72\%–8.06\%$, whereas 64 MiB DRAM drift remained around $\sim 23\%$.
- **Result:** **CONSISTENT WITH DRIFT MODERATION AT L1; DRAM VARIABILITY PERSISTS**
- *Epistemological Guardrail:* Because EXP-02 and EXP-03 used structurally different execution paradigms, the moderation in 32 KiB drift is an informative descriptive observation, **not causal proof** that separate process launches or interleaving alone were the governing factors.

## 9. Epistemological Guardrails and System Limitations

1. **Wall-Clock Amortized Timing:** All reported latencies reflect elapsed monotonic clock time (`std::chrono::steady_clock`) divided by the total number of completed pointer traversal operations. Sub-nanosecond times represent concurrent instruction throughput across processor pipelines, not sub-nanosecond physical DRAM access.
2. **Absence of Hardware Performance Counters (PMU):** Under Microsoft WSL2, direct hardware performance counters (`perf`, PMU events for cache misses, line-fill buffer occupancy, and memory controller traffic) are not exposed. We do not claim direct experimental observation of cache misses or memory bus transactions.
3. **Hypervisor & Frequency Scaling Effects:** Variance between blocks (such as the $\sim 23\%$ drift in 64 MiB traversal) reflects virtualized execution factors (thread scheduling, background host activity, core migration across Performance and Efficient cores, and DVFS frequency transitions) that cannot be controlled in this environment.
